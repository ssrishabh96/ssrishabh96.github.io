# My Response to the Proposed $103,265 H-1B Fee

*Rishabh Agrawal — October 2026*

The Department of Homeland Security has proposed charging **$103,265 for every cap-subject H-1B petition** (Federal Register doc 2026-17324, docket USCIS-2026-0298). The public comment period closed on September 24, 2026. This is my response as someone living under the system the fee would reshape.

## Who I am

I'm a Production Engineer at Meta in Menlo Park, building rate-limiting and capacity infrastructure. I came to the United States in August 2018 at age 24 on an F-1 visa, earned a master's degree, and have been on H-1B since 2022 — with an India EB-2 green-card path that means waiting years, not months.

I am exactly the person this fee is aimed at. And I want to be direct about what it would actually do.

## The fee doesn't filter for talent. It filters for employers who can write a check.

Supporters of the fee frame it as a way to stop abuse of the program. But a $103,265 filing fee doesn't distinguish between a staffing firm gaming the lottery and a mid-sized startup hiring its first infrastructure engineer. It does one thing: it prices smaller employers out of the H-1B entirely.

The result isn't fewer low-quality petitions — it's fewer petitions from anyone without a Big Tech balance sheet. The talent concentrates further into the handful of companies that can absorb the cost, and the rest of the economy loses access to the people it needs.

## The people it hits hardest are the ones who've already done everything right.

I did the degree. I did the legal process. I pay taxes here, I built a career here, I follow every rule of a system that takes years to navigate. The message of a six-figure fee is that doing it the right way is still not enough — that the door can be repriced at any moment for people who played by the rules.

That doesn't make the system stronger. It makes it arbitrary.

## What I'd propose instead

If the goal is program integrity, there are better tools than a blanket fee:

- **Enforce the rules that exist.** Go after the staffing firms and lottery abusers with audits and penalties — the fee punishes everyone for the sins of a few.
- **Fix the lottery mechanics.** A wage-weighted or skill-weighted selection would do more for integrity than any price tag.
- **Speed up legal immigration.** The India EB-2 backlog is measured in years. A fee that makes workers more expensive doesn't shorten a single queue.

## Closing

The United States has been the best place in the world to build ambitious things because it has historically been the best place in the world to *be* ambitious. A $103,265 entry fee for skilled workers doesn't protect that advantage — it taxes it.

I'm staying, and I'm going to keep building. But I'd rather the country I chose keeps choosing people like me back.

— *Rishabh Agrawal, Menlo Park, CA*
